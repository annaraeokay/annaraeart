Ha’azinu

הַאֲזִינוּ

A song at the edge of departure. Moses calls heaven and earth to listen, then gives Israel a poem meant to remain after his own voice is gone.

Date19 September 2026 · 8 Tishrei 5787
TorahDeuteronomy 32:1–52
ShabbatShabbat Shuvah · the Sabbath of Return
HaftarahHosea 14:2–10 · Joel 2:15–27

A thought for the week

Listening before returning.

“Give ear, O heavens, and I will speak; and let the earth hear the words of my mouth.” Deuteronomy 32:1

Ha’azinu begins with attention. Before correction, before memory, before return, Moses asks the world to listen.

The form matters. Near the end of his life, Moses does not leave Israel only another argument or instruction. He leaves a song. Poetry can carry what explanation cannot: warning, tenderness, grief, continuity, and the hope that a people will remember what they have heard long after the speaker is gone.

That feels especially sharp on Shabbat Shuvah, the Shabbat between Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. Return is not merely reversal. It asks for enough quiet to hear clearly, enough honesty to recognize where we are, and enough courage to move differently once we do.
